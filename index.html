First come, first served leaves some women behind

  • A woman in late pregnancy, or in a wheelchair, cannot walk to the road where the bus is waiting.
  • The nearest shelter may be full, cut off by water, or out of what she needs.
  • Many women have no smartphone, and the data network is the first thing to fail.

Move the water. Watch the order change.

This is the same scoring the console uses, run on the sample care list. As the river rises in the low-lying wards, women there move up. Someone bedridden and on oxygen stays near the top even when the water is low, because she cannot leave without help.

    How Aashray works

    1. Before the season, a health worker builds a care list

    She adds a woman only after the woman agrees. The list holds initials, ward, and what the woman needs: a stretcher, a wheelchair, a late pregnancy, a newborn, dialysis.

    2. When the alert is raised, the list ranks itself

    Each woman gets a score out of 100 from her medical need, pregnancy or newborn, mobility, age, how bad the flood is in her ward, and how far the nearest open shelter is. Tap "Why?" on any row to see the points. The coordinator can pin someone higher.

    3. Drivers get one text, and women can ask for help by SMS

    Each vehicle receives a single message listing its pickups and drop-off shelter. A woman without a smartphone can text HELP W3 P34 or give a missed call, and a person calls her back before anyone is sent.

    It is her information, so she stays in control

    She says yes first
    Nobody goes on the care list without her consent.
    Dispatchers see little
    Initials and ward only. Her address and phone go to the assigned driver, in the pickup text.
    Locked until it matters
    The list opens only while a flood alert is active, and every unlock is written to the audit log.
    She can leave any time
    One tap removes her record.
